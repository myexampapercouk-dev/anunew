Rest of World

Not one market but the parts of the industry moving fastest, everything outside the India, US, Europe, China and MENA corridors. South Korea's friend-shored biologics, Japan's innovator base, Singapore's advanced-manufacturing hub, and the emerging makers of Latin America and Southeast Asia. Here is how the corridor works when it is really a set of separate markets.

The hubs

A corridor made of several, not one

South Korea

exports $10.4B · 2025
biologics CDMO powerhouse

The centre of the ROW story. Samsung Biologics runs 785,000 litres in Songdo plus a new US site; Celltrion and Samsung Bioepis have made Korea the source of 14 US-FDA-approved biosimilars, second only to the US. Government policy targets $16B in exports and three global top-50 drugmakers by 2027. Under Section 232, Korean exports sit at the 15% tariff ceiling.

Japan

market $84.4B
largest market · innovator base

The region's largest market and a major innovator home to Takeda, Astellas, Daiichi Sankyo and Eisai. A net importer: it imported roughly four times what it exported in 2024.

Singapore

MNC biologics base
advanced-manufacturing hub

Small market, outsized manufacturing. A cluster of multinational biologics, mRNA and cell-and-gene plants, backed by strong government support and free-trade agreements.

Brazil

~33% of LatAm biologics
Latin America anchor

The largest Latin American market and the region's biggest biologics-manufacturing base, growing on domestic demand and biosimilars.

Australia, South Africa, ASEAN & the long tail

markets & emerging makers

Established markets and a spread of emerging manufacturing, each too small to anchor a corridor on its own today.

Each figure is for a single hub, from its own source. Rest of World is a grouping rather than a market, so it carries no single trade total.

How the corridor works

Position
Not a single market but the industry's fastest-moving edges outside the core corridors: friend-shored biologics capacity of East Asia, plus large Asian innovator and consumer markets.
Flows out
Biologics and biosimilars from South Korea, largely to the US and Europe; innovator drugs from Japan; contract-manufactured biologics from Singapore.
Flows in
Japan and most emerging markets are net importers of finished medicine and active ingredients. Korea imports its ingredients while exporting finished biologics.
Largest hubs
South Korea for the manufacturing ascent, Japan for market size (about $84B), Singapore for advanced-modality capacity, Brazil for Latin America.
Regulator gates
Japan's PMDA, Korea's MFDS, Singapore's HSA and Brazil's ANVISA, with US FDA and EMA qualification for exporters.
Open risk
US Section 232 tariffs, with Korea and Japan at the 15% ceiling, and whether Korea and Singapore keep winning the biologics work relocating out of China.
On the structure
This corridor is a grouping of markets, not a bloc. As its biggest stories mature, South Korea above all, they graduate into their own corridors and Rest of World narrows to the long tail.
